FARPO: Folding-Aware Reward-Decoupled Policy Optimization for Long-Horizon Mobile GUI Agents

Anonymous ICLR 2027 submission · project page

A Context-as-Action (ConAct) policy folds part of its own history, updates a UI memory and emits the next GUI action in one response. We post-train such a policy with reinforcement learning and find that the standard GRPO recipe fails in two ways. FARPO (Folding-Aware Reward-Decoupled Policy Optimization) standardises every verifier component within its prompt group before weighting, and sets the ratio of span to step folds in the training prompts explicitly. Starting from MemGUI-8B-SFT, one epoch of FARPO gives MemGUI-8B-RL: 48.4% Pass@3 and 39.0% information retention on MemGUI-Bench and 19.7% success on the out-of-distribution MobileWorld, the best open-data 8B model on both benchmarks.

01 / Overview

Where the GRPO recipe breaks on policy-managed memory

The verifier scores a ConAct response on four components (format, action type, action parameters, folding range); the folds it scores are mostly single-step. Both facts get in the way of group-relative policy optimisation.

Observation 1 Heterogeneous rewards need an explicit comparison scale.

Aggregating the components before group normalisation gives each one an effective weight proportional to its within-group spread. The format component is saturated (group mean ≈ 0.999) while the folding component is not (0.33 at initialisation), so the weights written into the objective are not the weights the update obeys.

Observation 2 Memory compression needs controlled exposure.

A span fold compresses several past steps and is the operation that shrinks the context; span folds are 22.7% of the annotated folds, and the majority step folds are almost always already correct. Under natural sampling the share of prompt groups that still carry a learning signal falls from about 70% to 23–38% within one epoch, and the policy collapses to step-only folding (deep-fold rate on held-out states 17.7% → 0.2%).

FARPO Two mechanisms, one per observation.

Within a prompt group, each component is standardised separately (as in GDPO) and only then weighted, which makes the advantage group-centred and scale-invariant. Across prompts, every span fold is kept and ⌈Nspan/ρ⌉ step folds are subsampled, so span exposure is one explicit ratio ρ; the released model uses ρ = 9 (deep-fold rate 92.5%).

03 / Method

The ConAct loop and the FARPO training step

Training is offline: a prompt is an annotated state of MemGUI-3K, the policy samples G = 5 responses, and an annotation-based verifier scores each one. No device rollouts are needed during training; task success is measured afterwards on the benchmarks.

Reward-decoupled group advantages

Each verifier component is centred and scaled within its prompt group, gated when its group variance is zero, and only then weighted (w = 0.1 / 0.4 / 0.4 / 0.1 for format, type, parameters, folding). A one-standard-deviation change in any component moves the advantage by the same amount, which the scalar recipe does not guarantee. Added to the GRPO recipe on the same natural corpus, it gives +6.2 Pass@3 and +2.8 IRR, and memory-argument match on the held-out states rises stepwise along the ladder (63.1 → 72.3 → 74.7 → 75.9).

Folding-aware sampling

Span folds are rare and step folds are already solved by the SFT policy, so under natural sampling the share of prompt groups with zero advantage grows from 25–33% to 62–77% over an epoch; with ρ = 9 it stays at 13–15%. The deep-fold rate on held-out states shows the two modes: natural corpora drive it to 0.2%, folding-aware corpora above 90%. At deployment MemGUI-8B-RL folds about 22 steps per span (SFT: 11) and writes 1.69 memory entries per attempt (SFT: 1.26).
